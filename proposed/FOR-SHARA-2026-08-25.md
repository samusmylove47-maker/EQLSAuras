## 2. Notes from the lockout module, carried across

Session D has been building a raid-lockout tracker as a standalone module, and wrote up integration
notes after reading how =Auras is already built. I have read all of it. Their own framing is the
right one and I will not improve on it: *"Nothing here is a request."* If the module never suits,
the measurements stand on their own.

Three findings about the **one-time full-log scan** you had planned. Two of them would give a
**wrong** answer rather than a missing one, which is why they are worth having before the code is
written rather than after.

### Scan the folder, not the newest file

The two halves of the only reset measurement they have live in **different files** — three task
grants on Monday 10 August in one file, three more on Tuesday the 11th in another. Scan only the
current file and you find three grants of three *different* tasks, no repeat, and the module
correctly reports "not recorded" having been shown exactly half the evidence.

**This one lands on you twice over, and Session D could not have known why.** They wrote it as a
warning about logs that happen to roll over. Your `logSplitter.js` writes per-day files *by design*,
continuously. So it is not a risk you might run into — it is the normal behaviour of your own
splitter, every day. Anything that reads history has to read the folder.

### One engine per character

A task is granted per character, and your watcher deliberately follows whichever `eqlog_*.txt`
changed most recently — right for buffs, wrong for this, and a folder scan picks up every character
on the machine.

When they first ran the corpus with one shared state, Avenrae's and your grants — four seconds
apart, because you were grouped — read as *one task granted twice*, and the module reported a
four-second reset bracket. `createState(character)` now refuses to be shared.

### It is fast, but stream it

Measured: **434 MB, 5,253,948 lines across 15 files, in 7.0 seconds.** A single character's Logs
folder is far smaller, so the backfill button is a few seconds, not a progress-bar-and-cancel
affair.

Use `readline` over `createReadStream` rather than `readFileSync` — one of those files is 112 MB,
and reading it synchronously on the main process spikes memory and blocks the window.

### One more, from their notes, about your tailer

They noticed that `logWatcher._pollActiveFile` opens a fresh `createReadStream` at a **byte** offset
with `encoding: 'utf8'` every 200 ms, so a multi-byte character straddling a poll boundary decodes
to U+FFFD. **I checked this against your code and it is correct** — the read is bounded
`start: this.offset, end: stat.size - 1`, and `this.lineBuffer` holds a partial *line*, already
decoded, so it cannot repair a split *character*.

It is not biting you: your corpus is effectively ASCII, and they measured exactly 9 bytes ≥ 0x80
across all 434 MB. Reading the tail as a `Buffer` and decoding after joining with the held partial
line closes it permanently. Small, latent, entirely yours to leave alone.

They also corrected a standing ruling of their own in the same document — these logs are UTF-8, not
Windows-1252 — after finding their earlier check had been piped through `grep`, which strips the
file's terminator and appends its own. Your `split(/\r\n|\n/)` already handles CRLF correctly, so
nothing changes on your side.

The full document is `docs/FOR-AURAS.md` in their repository if you want it.

## 3. The client prints lockout state after all — and one trap in reading it

This may change what a lockout feature is worth, so it is worth knowing before anything is designed
around the weaker version.

**What was found.** Four raids were run and the Alt+Z window captured. It lists **"Outstanding
Instance Timers"** — the per-boss lockout state everyone, us included, had concluded the client
never exposed. Two kinds of row: roughly 58-minute *Replay Timer* entries for instance re-entry, and
about 28 rows at 5d:23h:58m naming individual bosses.

**The loot lockout looks like a six-day rolling timer from when the loot is taken**, not the weekly
reset that everyone including us had assumed. The weekly *task* and the loot *lockout* are two
different objects — Session D declined to merge them on measured evidence, and this vindicates that.

**The open question is whether a chat command prints this to the log.** If it does, a tracker stops
inferring and starts reading, and becomes exact rather than probabilistic. That is a different
feature with a different value, which is the reason for telling you now rather than later.

### What your own logs say about it

I searched all 1,521,971 lines of your logs.

- **`dzlisttimers` and "Outstanding Instance Timers" appear zero times.** So does any `/dz` command,
  from anyone, in any channel. That means you have never typed it — not that it does not work.
- **Slash-command output does reach your log**: 180 `Usage: /cast` lines and 3 `Usage: /memspellset`.
  So *if* a command prints the table, it would almost certainly be logged. That is what makes the
  test below meaningful, and a negative result actually informative.
- Twice, players in General ask how to see lockouts. Both times the answer is the window, not a
  command — *"alt+z for raid lockout timers"* and, to "What is the command for your lockout menu?",
  simply *"alt z"*. Weak evidence, and I am giving it its honest weight: it suggests no
  widely-known printing command, and it does not rule one out.

**The ten-second test, if you are curious:** type the command in game, then look at the end of
today's log file. Because ordinary command output is logged, text appearing there settles it
outright, and nothing appearing is real evidence of a negative rather than an absence of data.

### The trap, which is the part I would most want you to have

**"Replay Timer" already appears in your logs 37 times, and none of them is a timer.**

Every one is the instance invitation:

```
Avenrae has asked you to join the instance: Befallen 1 (Awakened).
        Would you like to join? Accepting will incur you a charge or replay timer.
```

The only thing separating that from the Alt+Z rows is **capitalisation** — 37 lowercase
`replay timer`, zero title-case `Replay Timer`. And your custom-timer `contains` mode is
case-insensitive by design, for good reasons that have nothing to do with this. So a trigger built
on the words "replay timer" would fire on every instance invitation you receive and look, from the
outside, exactly like it was working.

Thin reeds like capitalisation are worth knowing about before something is built on one.

### Something usable regardless

That invitation line is unambiguous and already in your logs. It names the zone and the tier
exactly, 26 distinct combinations so far — `Befallen 1 (Awakened)`, `Blackburrow 2 (Adaptive)`,
`The Ruins of Old Guk 1 (Awakened)` — and it needs no inference at all. If instance tracking is ever
of interest, that shape is measured and sitting there. It is the only instance-related signal in
your corpus; there is no matching "you have joined" confirmation line to pair it with.
